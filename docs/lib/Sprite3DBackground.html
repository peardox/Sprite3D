Unit Sprite3DBackground

Description

A background for a Gorilla3D view: none, a checker for transparency, a colour or a gradient.

TSprite3DBackgroundSpec describes a background: its kind, its colours and the size of the checker squares. TSprite3DBackground draws one behind everything a TSprite3DCamera sees. It is an unlit plane that moves with the camera, just inside the far plane, and is resized every frame to fill the view exactly, whatever the projection, field of view or window size. Its colour comes from a short GLSL SurfaceShader: one colour for Solid, a blend down the plane for Gradient, with the plane turned in the view, and enlarged to cover it, to point the blend in the chosen GradientDirection. On macOS and iOS, where Gorilla3D draws through Metal and does not support a material's SurfaceShader, the colour is the material's own instead, and the gradient a small texture. It hangs from the camera's RollPivot, the dummy the camera itself hangs from; every plane is a pair facing opposite ways, so one always faces the camera however Gorilla culls back faces.

The None kind, the default, draws nothing at all, leaving whatever the viewport shows behind the scene, and grabs transparent. The Transparent kind draws a checker of two greys, the usual sign that an image is transparent there. It is built from geometry: the whole-view plane is put away, and two pools of small planes, one sharing a material of the light grey and one of the dark grey, all made before the first frame, tile the view side by side at the background's depth. They never overlap, so the depth buffer never has to choose between two surfaces at almost the same depth. Each frame only moves them, as the scene is never changed in any other way once it is drawn. The squares are CheckerSize logical pixels on screen, counted from the view's top-left corner, which is light. To keep the number of planes reasonable, a checker that would need more than MaxCheckerSquares squares of either grey has its squares enlarged by a whole multiple until it fits. Every plane is left out of Gorilla's frustum culling, which wrongly skipped most of the squares.

Every material has a black Diffuse colour: even unlit, Gorilla adds part of it to the colour a shader gives, which would brighten the background.

The same spec is meant for TSprite3DOffscreen.Backdrop, which gives grabs the same background: transparent for None and for Transparent, as the checker stands for, and the colour or gradient otherwise. Hide the background while a grab is drawn, with Hidden, so it does not end up in the grab.

Description

Types

TSprite3DBackgroundKind = (...);

What a background shows.

Values
  • None: No background is drawn: the viewport's own shows, and grabs are transparent. The default.
  • Transparent: A checker on screen, standing for transparency; transparency in grabs.
  • Solid: One colour, Colour.
  • Gradient: A blend from GradientFrom to GradientTo, in the direction GradientDirection.
TSprite3DGradientDirection = (...);

Which way a Gradient background runs, from GradientFrom to GradientTo.

The diagonals run corner to corner whatever the view's shape: the colour is constant along lines parallel to the other diagonal, as in CSS's to bottom right.

Values
  • TopToBottom: From the top edge to the bottom edge. The default.
  • LeftToRight: From the left edge to the right edge.
  • TopLeftToBottomRight: From the top-left corner to the bottom-right corner.
  • TopRightToBottomLeft: From the top-right corner to the bottom-left corner.

Declaration

type TSprite3DAnimationFrames = class(TSprite3DFrameSource)

Description

One cycle of a Gorilla model's animation, resampled at a chosen frame rate.

The animation's length D seconds is split into N equal steps, where N is D times the chosen rate, rounded, and at least 1. Frame I shows the pose at time I * D / N, so frame 0 is the first pose and frame N - 1 is one step before the end. Played at N frames per D seconds the frames take exactly as long as the animation, and since the time after the last frame is D, which is where a cyclical animation is back at its first pose, the frames loop without a jump or a repeated frame. The rate actually played, N / D, is the chosen rate adjusted by at most half a frame per cycle so the cycle stays whole; 8 frames a second on a 1.3-second walk gives 10 frames of 0.13 seconds.

Sprite3D loads models with vertex caching, so Gorilla has already baked the animation into FrameCount poses at its own rate (60 a second on desktop, 24 on some mobile platforms). Each output time is mapped onto that grid and the nearest baked pose is shown with SetFixedCacheFrame, whatever the animation clock is doing; on desktop that is within 1/120 of a second of the exact time. The poses themselves are interpolated between the file's keyframes when Gorilla bakes them; the images are never blended, which would leave ghosts in a sprite. At a rate above the baked rate some poses repeat.

An animation without baked poses is instead posed by seeking its skeleton to each exact time, which interpolates the keyframes there. On Delphi 13 and later, where Gorilla's render clock holds the skeleton, the pose is evaluated on the next drawn frame, so it can lag the requested time by one display frame.

Animations that do not cycle are not treated differently yet: their last pose is left out, as for a cycle.

Start makes the animation the model's current one, without blending, and starts it.

Description

Grabs every frame of a model's animation, or of a turn of the camera round it, one off-screen grab after another.

A frame source says how many frames there are, how long they last together, and how to show frame N. TSprite3DAnimationFrames shows the frames of one of a Gorilla model's animations, from the first to the last; TSprite3DTurntableFrames turns a TSprite3DCamera once round the model, for a model or a moment with no animation to play.

TSprite3DFrameCapture then shows each frame in turn and grabs it with a TSprite3DOffscreen, starting the next grab from inside the previous one's procedure, as TSprite3DOffscreen.GrabSizes does. The grabs are copied into TSprite3DImage records straight away, so the whole run holds no FMX bitmaps, and the caller gets them all at the end in one procedure, ready for Sprite3DSpriteSheet to lay out and save.

The capture does not frame the model; the owner of the camera sets one framing for the whole run before starting it, so every frame is drawn at the same scale, and puts its own back afterwards.

A newly started animation can take a few drawn frames to show its first pose: Gorilla switches clips, uploads the baked poses and settles the skeleton over the next frames drawn, and until then the model can be in the pose it had before, or at the wrong height. So the first frame is grabbed WarmUpFrames times, DEFAULT_WARM_UP_FRAMES unless changed, and those grabs are thrown away before the real one.

TSprite3DAnimationPlayer plays, pauses and steps an animation on screen, one export frame at a time, holding each pose as a capture would. SetAnimationPaused pauses an animation's clock the same way on every Delphi version.

Everything here is for the main thread. A capture runs over many frames of the main loop; the application stays responsive meanwhile, and should not start other grabs, load another model or change the animation until the capture reports.

procedure SetAnimationPaused(const AAnimation: TGorillaAnimation; const APaused: Boolean);

Holds an animation on its current pose, or lets it carry on from there.

Pauses or unpauses the animation's skeleton clock, the same way on every Delphi version: Skeleton.Paused on Delphi 13 and later, where Gorilla's render clock owns Pause, and Skeleton.Pause before. A paused animation stays current and running, with its pose held, baked or not, so whatever follows the playing animation still sees it; unpaused, it carries on from the same moment. Main thread only.

Parameters
AAnimation
The animation; nil, or one without a skeleton, is ignored.
APaused
True to hold the pose, False to carry on.
